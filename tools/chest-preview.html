What changed, and the one thing I would do differently

  • Six squares a page, as you said. Three across, two down. A page means the same thing on the phone and on the PC — the squares grow instead of multiplying, so "page 2" is never a different amount of goods on a different screen.
  • The product stands in the square as a real box, the same one the Master Vault and the sales terminal draw: its true width, height and depth in millimetres decide the shape, so a 16-stick pack really is fatter than a 12. Here the faces are flat colours standing in for the product photos the app already holds — in the app they are the photos.
  • Each box is placed, not spun. When the page opens every box turns once into place, one after another. Resting your mouse on a square turns that one box slowly; nothing turns for ever by itself, and on the phone nothing turns until you touch it.
  • The chest stays brown in both looks, as you asked. That is the same exemption the printed nota has: the chest is a thing, not app furniture.
  • Lite Mode and "reduce motion" keep every box, every number and every page — they just arrive without the turn.

Where I would push back, slightly: six squares is right for the phone, but Budi's van has nine products today, so he would be on page 2 most mornings. I would keep six — one page must mean one thing — and instead make the page buttons carry what is on them (a little "3" on page 2 when three products sit there), so nobody has to turn a page to find out whether it is worth turning. The prototype does that already: look under the squares.

The two you added: dragging, and the damaged row

  • Drag works with a finger, not only a mouse. Press a box, move it, drop it on another square; a press that barely moves is still a tap, so you can turn a box without moving it. The order is written once, when you let go — and the word "tersimpan" appears where the work happened, because every action must say so.
  • What gets saved is only the ORDER — a short list of which product sits in which square. It is not the stock. In the app it belongs in a small field of its own, never inside the van's contents, because the two stock transactions and every sale already write that; a tidy-up must not be able to collide with a sale.
  • The damaged row is always under the chest, with the reason on each one (Kemasan rusak · Basah · Pita cukai). That is what an admin could only see on the night's report before.
  • You cannot drag a good pack into the damaged row. That would mean "this came back broken", which is a stock movement with a receipt behind it, not a tidy-up. Try it — the chest says so out loud.
  • The chest IS the agent inventory for a regional admin and above, as you said. The salesman keeps his own screen; this is the same van seen from the office.

One cost to know before we build it: saving the order means a write every time someone rearranges. On its own that is cheap — but it must be a field of its own, and it must be written on drop rather than while dragging, or a busy morning turns into hundreds of writes and a chance to overwrite a sale that landed mid-drag.
